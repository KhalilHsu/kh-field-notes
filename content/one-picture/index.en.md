These days, whether traveling or just living daily life, everyone has gotten used to mashing the shutter button like a machine gun.

In an era of information overload, the marginal cost of taking a photo has dropped to zero. But opening my camera roll one day and staring at tens of thousands of pictures, I found it nearly impossible to pinpoint a single shot that genuinely meant something to me. We generate mountains of digital debris, and it leaves you feeling subtly exhausted.

It reminded me of my childhood, and the old 35mm film camera my father carried everywhere.

Back then, whenever he took a picture of me, I stood attentively and held my pose. He would pace back and forth, squinting to frame the light and angle just right. A roll of film held only thirty-six frames. In that era, every single press of the shutter felt weighty and deliberate.

---

At the end of May (May 28th), during my wedding leave in Yunnan, I vibe-coded this app with Codex during my downtime: **One Picture**.

![One Picture Workflow and Interaction Demo](ee.mov)

Its rule is uncompromisingly simple:

* **Every day**: you pick and save exactly one photo you consider the most meaningful of that day.

No algorithm feeds, no social metrics. It operates like a distillation filter, gently forcing you to pause and make one thoughtful choice before the day closes.

Since finishing it in late May, I’ve been using it quietly for several months. Now, whenever I flip through its roll, the moments that actually mattered—the crisp morning sunlight in Yunnan, a wild blossom by the roadside, an ordinary cup of coffee on a quiet afternoon—come rushing back with vivid clarity.

Photography shouldn’t be a cheap burst of clicks. One picture a day—saving the moments that truly matter for your future self.
