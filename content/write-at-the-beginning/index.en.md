At my core, I love talking, but I've always hated documenting.

Whenever a weird idea popped into my head or I hacked together some tiny project, I could ramble to friends about it for hours over drinks. But the moment I sat down to turn those thoughts into structured prose, clean layouts, and polished articles, the friction was suffocating. It killed all the excitement instantly.

Then the AI Agent era arrived, and the cost of documentation dropped to near zero. Now, I can just mutter casually into the mic on my desk or dump messy scratch scripts and brain-farts onto an agent, and it turns them into coherent, structured thoughts in seconds.

Since the friction of capturing things has basically vanished, why not carve out a quiet corner on the internet to keep these records? I want to see what kind of random shit I’ll end up tinkering with over the next few years.

---

When you strip it down, human motivation really boils down to just two things.

The first is **doing something to "get" something**.

Maybe it's to make a quick buck, feed your ego, chase clout, or win external validation. That's totally normal. Most gears of the modern world and commercial society turn on the certainty of expected returns.

The second is purely **out of habit, or because you love it from the bottom of your heart**.

This kind of drive is a lot like love—you can't explain it with airtight logic or clean ROI formulas. To outsiders, it often looks clumsy, even irrational. Like staying up until 4:00 AM hacking on a macOS menu bar tweak only you will ever care about. Or slipping a silicone ear onto a desktop mic just to give an AI a physical presence. Or writing an entire parser plugin just to organize data nobody else asked for.

None of this leads to what society calls "massive success," nor does it print money.

Yet whenever I pause and look back, I realize I genuinely, wholeheartedly love doing this stuff.

---

If I zoom out five or ten years, future me might open this blog and see an archive full of trivial, half-baked, seemingly "useless" tinkering logs.

But if that future self looks at all this and breaks into a quiet smile, then this site has served its purpose. Because it means that while being carried forward by the relentless grind of everyday life, I never lost that initial, uncorrupted joy of making things.

Written at the very beginning—a note to my future self.
